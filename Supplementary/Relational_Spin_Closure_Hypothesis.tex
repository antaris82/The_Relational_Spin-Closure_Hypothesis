\begin{abstract}
This paper states a research hypothesis and a falsification programme, not a completed physical theory.  Its mathematical baseline has changed substantially after the companion reconstruction paper, hereafter Paper I \cite{PaperI}.  The primitive local input is no longer a pre-existing Minkowski space or Lorentzian Spin bundle.  Paper I starts only from the standard positive-definite Euclidean space $V=\R^3$ and constructs the four-dimensional spin factor $\Smodel=\R\oplus V$, the Lorentzian quadratic form $N(a,v)=a^2-\langle v,v\rangle$, the real Clifford algebra $\Cl_3$, an intrinsic Spin group $\SpinG$, a surjective central double cover $\rho:\SpinG\to\LorG$ with kernel $\{\pm1\}$, and a six-dimensional Lorentz-skew Lie algebra $\gB\simeq\Lambda^2\Smodel$.

Paper I also fixes two dependency errors that any mature Relational Spin-Closure Hypothesis must respect.  First, local algebra does not determine a global spacetime base: explicit incidence/gluing data are additional.  Under a closed-gluing-graph condition, countability and smooth gluing, those data yield a smooth four-manifold $M$.  Second, the internal Lorentz cocycle is not automatically the derivative cocycle of $TM$: a regular solder is an additional gate.  Conditional on such a solder, one obtains a smooth Lorentzian tensor on $TM$, project-native orientation and future-cone reductions, and exact reconvergence of the tangent Lorentz cocycle with the projection of native Spin transition data.  These project-native obstruction statements are not identified with $w_1(TM)$ or $w_2(TM)$.

A selected transition-level deformation in Paper I gives an equally important negative result.  On a fixed periodic control, every finite deformation parameter is regularly admissible, all parameters lie in one full Spin Cech gauge orbit, and the complete regular-solder solution spaces are explicitly equivalent with unchanged induced tangent metric.  Thus a transition parameter that looks nontrivial before quotienting can have no closure-selection power on the tested control.  The updated RSCH therefore cannot take a raw transition parameter, or a gauge-dependent ``mismatch'', as a physical closure variable merely by declaration.

The hypothesis is now sharpened accordingly.  The next native mathematical edge is the infinitesimal Spin-to-Lorentz bridge: a Lie-group/Lie-algebra identification for the intrinsic Spin group and the differential $d\rho_e$.  Only thereafter can a native connection, parallel transport, holonomy, curvature and torsion be introduced without importing them as unexplained background structures.  The central physical question is then whether the currently exogenous global gates and the future connection-level geometry can be selected or constrained by one classical gauge-covariant reciprocal closure linking local/core state and relational geometry.  Quantization remains a later conditional extension and may not rescue failure of the classical closure.
\end{abstract}

\section{The certified baseline after Paper I}

\subsection{Primitive local input}
The local primitive is
\begin{equation}
V=\R^3,
\qquad
\langle u,v\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^3 u_i v_i,
\label{eq:primitive}
\end{equation}
with its standard positive-definite Euclidean form.  No four-dimensional manifold, tangent bundle, Lorentz metric, orientation, Spin bundle, connection, holonomy or curvature is included in \eqref{eq:primitive}.

From this datum Paper I constructs the real spin factor
\begin{equation}
\Smodel=\R\oplus V,
\qquad
N(a,v)=a^2-\langle v,v\rangle,
\label{eq:spinfactor}
\end{equation}
with signature $(1,3)$, together with the associated bilinear Lorentz form.  In parallel, the Euclidean Clifford algebra $\Cl(V,q)\cong\Cl_3$ is built from the same three-dimensional input.  The paravector embedding identifies the quadratic structure of \eqref{eq:spinfactor} with the Clifford construction in the precise sense proved in Paper I \cite{PaperI,Chevalley,LawsonMichelsohn}.

\subsection{Intrinsic Spin cover and infinitesimal Lorentz algebra}
Inside $\Cl_3$, Paper I defines an intrinsic Spin group $\SpinG$ and an intrinsically characterized proper cone-preserving Lorentz group $\LorG$.  The Clifford action gives a surjective group homomorphism
\begin{equation}
1\longrightarrow\{\pm1\}
\longrightarrow\SpinG
\overset{\rho}{\longrightarrow}\LorG
\longrightarrow1,
\label{eq:cover}
\end{equation}
with exact central kernel $\{\pm1\}$.  Both sides are established as Hausdorff topological groups, the cover is continuous, the kernel is discrete, and continuous local sections exist.

Independently, the Lorentz-skew endomorphisms of $\Smodel$ form a six-dimensional Lie algebra $\gB$ under commutator, and Paper I constructs a linear equivalence
\begin{equation}
\Lambda^2\Smodel\simeq\gB,
\qquad
\dim\gB=6.
\label{eq:bivector}
\end{equation}
A crucial boundary remains:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Lie}(\SpinG)\simeq\gB
\qquad\text{has not yet been established.}
\label{eq:lieopen}
\end{equation}
Consequently the differential $d\rho_e$ of the native cover is also open.

\subsection{Global base is additional information}
Local copies of $\Smodel$ do not determine which local points belong to one global base.  Paper I therefore introduces explicit base-gluing data: local domains, incidence domains and compatible identification maps.  The quotient constructed from these data is not automatically a manifold.  With a closed gluing graph and a countable index set it is Hausdorff and second countable; with an additional smooth-gluing condition the reconstructed atlas yields a smooth four-manifold $M$.

The key conceptual consequence is
\begin{equation}
\text{local Lorentz/Spin algebra}
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{unique global base}.
\label{eq:nobase}
\end{equation}
This is not a philosophical preference but a theorem-level non-derivability control: different admissible gluing data can assemble the same local pieces into different global spaces.

\subsection{Internal Lorentz data and tangent geometry are distinct}
Once a smooth base exists, its true tangent transitions are derivatives of chart changes,
\begin{equation}
T_{ij}=D\phi_{ij}.
\label{eq:tangenttransition}
\end{equation}
Native Spin transition data project through \eqref{eq:cover} to an internal Lorentz cocycle $g_{ij}$.  Paper I proves that these systems are not automatically identical.  A simple rescaling control already separates them.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\text{internal Lorentz cocycle}
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{tangent cocycle}.
\label{eq:notangent}
\end{equation}

Its existence is a gate.  Paper I does not prove that every admissible base and native Spin datum admits such a solder.  Explicit negative controls show that this would be false in the project-native setting.

\section{Terminology, reciprocity and the no-clock constraint}

\subsection{Relational Spin comparison}
The historical heuristic that motivated RSCH used language of ``synchronization''. None of those objects is part of the certified baseline or the revised hypothesis.

At the present formal level, \emph{relational Spin comparison} means the gauge-covariant information carried by native Spin transition data together with whatever solder information is required to compare that internal system with tangent geometry.  After the connection layer exists, the term may extend to connection-level comparison data.  In every case, the physical object is the appropriate equivalence class, not an arbitrary representative.

\begin{warning}[No clock variable]

\end{warning}

\subsection{Reciprocity is constitutive, not retrocausal}
The word \emph{reciprocal} means that one solution relation constrains local/core state and relational geometry jointly.  It does not mean backward causation in an external time variable.  Near a regular classical solution $z_0=(\Phi_0,G_0)$ of a future closure law, with $G$ denoting the surviving geometric variables after all gauge reductions, one may linearize schematically as
\begin{equation}
D\mathfrak C_{z_0}(\delta\Phi,\delta G)
=C_\Phi\,\delta\Phi+C_G\,\delta G.
\label{eq:linearclosure}
\end{equation}
After gauge zero-modes and boundary conditions are handled, a strong local reciprocity test asks whether state-driven perturbations admit compensating geometric responses and geometry-driven perturbations admit compensating state responses within the same solution manifold.  When the relevant partial operators are invertible on the tested subspaces, the linear template is
\begin{align}
\delta G&=-C_G^{-1}C_\Phi\,\delta\Phi,\\
\delta\Phi&=-C_\Phi^{-1}C_G\,\delta G.
\end{align}
This is a diagnostic template, not a claim that the eventual nonlinear theory has globally invertible derivatives.

\begin{criterion}[One-law criterion]
Different boundary or initial perturbations are allowed.  Two unrelated fundamental response laws, one for state-to-geometry and one for geometry-to-state, do not satisfy the central RSCH unification claim.
\end{criterion}

\subsection{Variational closure is a stronger candidate, not an assumption}
A particularly economical future realization would be a single classical gauge-invariant action whose simultaneous stationarity equations determine the state and geometric sectors.  Such a variational formulation would automatically package reciprocity into one constitutive principle.  It is, however, a stronger subhypothesis.  The programme must not insert the Einstein--Hilbert, Palatini, Einstein--Cartan or Yang--Mills action and then advertise the resulting equations as a derivation of RSCH.  Kibble's gauge-gravity construction is therefore a benchmark and control, not a premise \cite{Kibble1961}.

The standard torsion-free Levi--Civita/geodesic branch is a terminal comparison, not a premise.  A future connection-level RSCH may instead contain torsion or other degrees of freedom, but any surviving branch must satisfy observational constraints.

\section{Localized structures, radiation and later particle language}

If a classical reciprocal closure is found, one may search its solution space for stable localized configurations.  A classical localized-solution candidate must be non-gauge, finite in the conserved quantities defined by the theory, asymptotic to an admissible background and stable or metastable under the actual dynamics.  Skyrme's nonlinear field model provides a historical example of particle-like classical solitons, but not evidence that RSCH possesses them \cite{Skyrme1961}.

Mass should be assigned only after a solution and suitable asymptotic Poincare charges exist.  In an asymptotically Poincare-invariant sector, the invariant extracted from total four-momentum is then a classifier of the solution, not a primitive source used to create it.

Likewise, a propagating classical mode is not yet a photon.  The sequence must be
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{classical physical mode}
&\longrightarrow \text{consistent quantization}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{quantum representation/statistics}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{particle interpretation}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
A longitudinal feature of an internal classical variable is not by itself a longitudinal observed photon.

\section{Generation and strong-sector extensions are late branches}

The hypothesis does not predict three generations at the present stage.  A generation mechanism would require a mature solution/classification problem in which distinct stable branches with otherwise matching observable quantum numbers arise without inserting the number three.

Similarly, the Lorentz Spin group is not a colour gauge group.  A QCD comparison is admissible only if an independent non-Abelian internal gauge sector is derived and equipped with gauge-invariant observables.  Wilson loops, confinement criteria, asymptotic freedom and dense/deconfined phases then become demanding benchmarks \cite{YangMills1954,Wilson1974,GrossWilczek1973,Politzer1973,Shuryak1978}.  Formal resemblance to non-Abelian transport does not establish QCD.


\section{The two-sided smoke test after Paper I}

The original RSCH programme proposed a deliberately small reciprocity test before attempting a full continuum dynamics.  Paper I now supplies an important preliminary result: one selected transition-level family has already failed to provide an independent physical closure knob because it is fully gauge absorbable on the tested periodic control.  The smoke-test protocol is therefore refined rather than discarded.

\subsection{Control 0: transition-level gauge absorption -- completed negative control}
The selected one-parameter Spin transition family must remain in the test suite permanently.  Any future closure functional that reports a physical geometric distinction along this family must explain why that distinction survives the explicit full-group Cech gauge equivalence and the metric-preserving solder-space correspondence.  If it cannot, the proposed observable is reading gauge redundancy.

\subsection{Control 1: state-side perturbation}
After a classical state sector $\Phi$ has been defined, begin from a neutral soldered/connection-level background and perturb only admissible state-side boundary or initial data.  Do not input a velocity law, redshift profile or target force law.  The closure must determine whether and how the relational geometry readjusts.  In the flat branch, the terminal comparison is special-relativistic kinematics and, once operational state observables exist, special-relativistic dynamics.

\subsection{Control 2: geometry-side perturbation}
On the same candidate closure law, perturb admissible geometric boundary data while applying no independent direct force impulse to the local/core state.  The state response must arise through the same constitutive relation.  At this stage an externally imposed geometric perturbation is permitted only as a diagnostic boundary condition; it is not yet the claimed endogenous background.

\subsection{Control 3: many-body endogenous replacement}
Replace the externally imposed geometric perturbation by a localized many-core configuration and solve the state and geometric sectors simultaneously.  A remote test configuration is then evolved in the resulting self-consistent geometry.  This is the first smoke test that addresses the intended gravitational mechanism rather than a programmed background.

\begin{criterion}[Smoke-test consistency]
Controls 1--3 must use one and the same closure law and one and the same gauge quotient.  Retuning the constitutive law between the flat, geometry-driven and many-body tests invalidates the unification claim.  Control 0 remains a mandatory negative control for every candidate transition-level observable.
\end{criterion}

\section{Updated research programme}

The post-Paper-I programme is best organized by structural edges rather than historical task numbers.

\subsection{Stage A: certified conditional Lorentzian Spin geometry -- complete}
Paper I establishes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item Euclidean $\R^3$ primitive $\to$ Lorentz spin factor, Clifford algebra, intrinsic Spin cover and $\gB$;
\item explicit base-gluing layer and sufficient topological/smooth gates $\to M^4$;
\item exact separation of internal Lorentz and genuine tangent cocycles;
\item regular solder as the additional gate $\to$ Lorentzian tangent metric and project-native reconvergence;
\item positive and negative global controls;
\item a selected transition deformation whose apparent parameter is fully gauge absorbable on the tested control and metrically inert under the full solder-space correspondence.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage B: intrinsic infinitesimal Spin--Lorentz bridge -- next}
\textbf{Goal:} construct the missing native infinitesimal bridge without importing an external matrix Lie group.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item package the intrinsic $\SpinG$ with sufficient smooth/Lie structure;
\item prove or refute $\operatorname{Lie}(\SpinG)\simeq\gB$;
\item construct $d\rho_e$ and verify its action on the intrinsic Lorentz algebra;
\item keep the distinction between the proven abstract $\gB$ and the not-yet-proven Lie algebra of $\SpinG$ explicit.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Failure signal:} if the native topological Spin group cannot support the required smooth/Lie structure or its Lie algebra fails to match the expected six-dimensional sector, the intended connection branch must be reformulated.

\subsection{Stage C: native connection geometry}
\textbf{Goal:} introduce a principal Spin/Lorentz connection only after Stage B.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item define native connection data on the certified global bundle structure;
\item construct covariant derivative and parallel transport;
\item prove the finite-holonomy and infinitesimal-curvature relations in the native setting;
\item introduce torsion only after solder and connection coexist;
\item compare project-native orientation/Spin obstructions with standard characteristic classes where possible.
\end{enumerate}
This stage is standard geometry adapted to the native reconstruction.  It is still not RSCH dynamics.

\subsection{Stage D: closure-variable audit}
\textbf{Goal:} determine what survives the gauge quotient and is capable of nontrivial reciprocal selection.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item classify gauge-invariant transition-, solder- and connection-level observables;
\item reuse the Paper-I deformation as a mandatory negative control;
\item reject candidate variables that are pure gauge or metrically inert on the relevant quotient;
\item determine the minimal argument list for a possible $\mathfrak C$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage E: finite reciprocal closure search}
\textbf{Goal:} search a constrained classical family of closure laws in a finite or otherwise controllable many-core setting.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item vary relational/core state and geometric data independently at the boundary;
\item require both responses to arise from one law;
\item demand a neutral gauge-invariant sector and a genuinely nontrivial collective sector;
\item record negative results rather than retuning the target into the law.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage F: classical flat-sector dynamics}
\textbf{Goal:} add a genuine classical state sector and recover special-relativistic observables in the neutral geometry without a preferred frame.

\subsection{Stage G: self-generated weak geometry}
\textbf{Goal:} demonstrate that many-core configurations can generate a viable long-range geometric response and free-fall phenomenology without importing GR field equations.

\subsection{Stage H: localized states and scales}
\textbf{Goal:} classify stable classical localized solutions, conserved charges and scale provenance; only then ask whether invariant mass and repeated branches emerge.

\subsection{Stage I: conditional quantization}
\textbf{Goal:} identify a reduced phase space or equivalent structure and attempt quantization only after the classical closure has independently survived the flat and gravitational gates.

\subsection{Stage J: quantum/strong-sector extensions}
\textbf{Goal:} particle, generation, radiation, confinement and dense-phase questions are opened only if the required intermediate structures have actually emerged.

\section{Hard falsifiers}

\begin{falsifier}[No nontrivial quotient-level closure]
If every candidate classical closure on the correctly quotient configuration space is either trivial, pure gauge, or equivalent to importing the target field equations, the central RSCH fails.
\end{falsifier}

\begin{falsifier}[Two-law failure]
If state-to-geometry and geometry-to-state responses require irreducibly independent fundamental laws with unrelated parameters rather than two boundary realizations of one constitutive structure, the reciprocal-unification claim fails.
\end{falsifier}

\begin{falsifier}[Gate erasure]
If the programme can proceed only by pretending that global base gluing or regular solder existence follows automatically from the local Spin/Clifford algebra, it contradicts the certified dependency structure and the proposed derivation fails.
\end{falsifier}

\begin{falsifier}[Connection-by-relabeling]
If finite transition products are simply renamed holonomy or curvature without constructing the native connection-level bridge and proving the required continuum comparison, the claimed geometric derivation has not occurred.
\end{falsifier}

\begin{falsifier}[Quantum rescue]
If the classical closure fails its existence, reciprocity, SRT or endogenous-geometry tests and target behaviour appears only after adding quantum postulates or a different quantum dynamical law, the classical RSCH has failed.
\end{falsifier}

\begin{falsifier}[Matter extension failure]
If the eventual closure has no stable localized finite-energy solutions, the proposed matter branch fails even if the geometric branch survives.
\end{falsifier}

\begin{falsifier}[Strong-sector extension failure]
If no appropriate independent internal non-Abelian gauge sector emerges, no confinement or QGP claim follows from RSCH.  If such a sector emerges but fails its gauge-invariant strong-interaction benchmarks, that extension is falsified.
\end{falsifier}

\section{Relation to established literature}

Paper I is the immediate mathematical baseline for this revision \cite{PaperI}.  Its local constructions sit in the standard mathematical landscape of spin factors, Clifford algebras and Spin groups \cite{Chevalley,LawsonMichelsohn,FarautKoranyi}.  Its global reconstruction uses standard quotient/manifold and bundle ideas while reversing the usual order of assumptions \cite{LeeSmooth,Husemoller,Steenrod}.

Nakahara's \emph{Geometry, Topology and Physics} is used here as a standard-control source for the later geometric target architecture -- principal bundles, connections, holonomy, curvature, coframes and Spin geometry -- and not as evidence for the new closure \cite{Nakahara2003}.  Kobayashi and Nomizu provide the corresponding differential-geometric bundle framework \cite{KobayashiNomizu}.  Cartan, Fock, Weyl and Kibble are historical controls for torsion, local frames, spinorial gravity and gauge-gravity constructions \cite{Cartan1923,Fock1929,Weyl1929,Kibble1961}.  The proposed novelty is not any one of these structures; it is the still-unproved possibility of an endogenous reciprocal selection law native to the reconstructed dependency chain.

Einstein and Schwarzschild define central macroscopic gravity benchmarks, not premises \cite{Einstein1916,Schwarzschild1916}.  Yang--Mills, Wilson, Gross--Wilczek, Politzer and Shuryak delimit what would have to be recovered before later strong-interaction language becomes legitimate \cite{YangMills1954,Wilson1974,GrossWilczek1973,Politzer1973,Shuryak1978}.

\section{Conclusion}

Paper I substantially narrows and strengthens the Relational Spin-Closure Hypothesis.  The verified local starting point is not Minkowski space but positive Euclidean three-space.  From it one can reconstruct a four-dimensional Lorentz spin factor, a Clifford envelope, an intrinsic Spin double cover and a six-dimensional Lorentz-skew algebra.  A global base still requires explicit gluing information.  Tangent geometry still requires a regular solder.  These are not defects of the reconstruction; they are the precise places where genuinely global information enters.

The first transition-deformation smoke test then adds a second lesson.  Nontrivial-looking Spin transition data can be globally gauge absorbable on a concrete control, while the complete solder solution spaces remain equivalent and the induced tangent metric remains unchanged.  Therefore transition-level variation is not automatically physical geometry.  Future closure variables must be defined after the relevant gauge quotient and tested against this negative control.

The current mathematical frontier is consequently sharp:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Paper-I conditional Lorentzian Spin geometry}
&\longrightarrow \operatorname{Lie}(\SpinG),\ d\rho_e\\
&\longrightarrow \text{native connection geometry}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{candidate reciprocal closure}.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:frontier}
\end{equation}
Only the first box in \eqref{eq:frontier} is currently complete.  The central RSCH conjecture begins beyond it: that one classical gauge-covariant law can eventually select or constrain the relational background and local/core state reciprocally, without inserting known gravitational equations and without relying on quantum postulates.

This formulation is deliberately falsifiable.  The hypothesis may fail because no nontrivial quotient-level closure exists, because the missing Lie/connection bridge cannot be made native, because the flat sector fails special relativity, because collective configurations fail to generate viable geometry, or because later matter and quantum extensions do not exist.  Each failure is allowed to terminate the corresponding branch rather than being repaired by redefining the target.
